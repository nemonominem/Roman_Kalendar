# The Roman Kalendar

A single-page interactive explainer of the Roman calendar — from its lunar origins through the Republican 355-day year, Caesar's 46 BC reform, and the naming of months and days — with an emphasis on *why* it worked the way it did, not just a list of facts.

**Live site:** https://nemonominem.github.io/Roman_Kalendar/

## What's here

The page is organized into tabs:

- **The Kalendar** — a full-year Roman ↔ modern date converter, with key events and festivals.
- **Lunar Origin** — the moon-phase roots of Kalends/Nones/Ides, and why the calendar stopped tracking the sky.
- **Rhythm of Time** — the calendar as civic/religious/financial operating system; the nundinal (market-day) cycle.
- **Month Lengths** — five snapshots of month lengths across history, from Romulus's traditional 304-day year to the Julian reform.
- **Day Names** — how Romans counted backwards from Kalends, Nones, and Ides.
- **Month Names** — where each month's name comes from.
- **Fasti** — the religious day-letter system (F/N/C/NP/EN) and the festival year.
- **Caesar's Reform** — the 445-day *annus confusionis* and the Julian calendar.
- **Consuls** — dating by consular pairs, and how that system broke down.
- **Examples** — real ancient dating in practice: Cicero, Augustus, Pliny, and inscriptions on stone.
- **Quiz** — a Gregorian → Roman date converter and a 12-question quiz.
- **Notes** — sourcing, uncertainties, and reconstruction choices.

## Tech

A single self-contained `index.html` — vanilla JS and CSS, no build step, no dependencies. Fonts are loaded from Google Fonts (Cinzel, EB Garamond). Deployed automatically to GitHub Pages on every push to `main` (see `.github/workflows/`).
